
# Aluminium Powder Coating: A Guide to Masking Functional Areas

Aluminium powder coating gives metal parts a coloured, finished surface. However, some areas of a component may need to remain free of coating so the part can be assembled or perform its intended function.

This is why masking should be discussed before aluminium powder coating begins.

Masking means covering selected areas before coating so that powder does not reach them. The areas to mask depend on the component design, its use and the project requirements.

## Why Is Masking Important?

[Powder coating](https://varunautocoat.com/aluminium-powder-coating/) adds a layer to the surface of a part. If coating reaches an area that needs a specific fit, thread or electrical contact, it may affect how that part works.

For example, a coated profile may look correct but still create an assembly problem if a critical fitting area was not protected.

A masking plan helps the fabricator and coating supplier agree on which areas need protection before production starts.

## 1. Threaded Holes and Fastener Areas

Threads are designed to fit matching screws, bolts or other fasteners.

If powder coating builds up inside a thread, fitting the fastener may become difficult. The coating could also be damaged during assembly.

Before sending a component for coating, identify any threaded holes or fastener areas that must remain clear.

Ask the coating supplier to confirm a suitable masking method for the part and the coating process.

## 2. Close-Fitting and Mating Surfaces

Some aluminium components must fit closely against another part.

Examples may include selected assembly faces, precision-fitting areas and surfaces where a specified clearance must be maintained.

Coating thickness can affect these dimensions. If a surface must remain uncoated, or if the coating thickness is limited, identify that requirement on the drawing or job specification.

Do not assume that every mating surface should be left bare. The correct decision depends on the component design and the required finish.

## 3. Electrical Contact Areas

Some components need direct electrical contact at specific points.

An insulating powder coating on such a contact area may interfere with that function. These areas may therefore require masking or another approved finishing arrangement.

The required contact points should be clearly identified before coating. The fabricator and coating supplier should agree on the method and how the finished part will be checked.

## 4. Areas That Need a Specific Fit

Not every component has the same dimensional requirements.

A part may have a slot, recess, locating face or other feature that must fit another component. In these cases, the effect of coating build-up should be considered during design and production planning.

The supplier should be told which dimensions or surfaces are critical. Where necessary, confirm whether masking, a suitable coating allowance or another approved approach is required.

Do not assume that coating can be removed later without affecting the part or its appearance.

## 5. How Should Masking Requirements Be Shared?

Clear instructions reduce the risk of misunderstanding between fabrication and coating teams.

Before production, provide:

- A drawing that identifies the areas to be masked.
- The required colour and finish.
- Any critical dimensions or fit requirements.
- Details of threaded holes or electrical contact points.
- The required coating specification, where applicable.
- Any inspection or acceptance requirements.

If a drawing is unclear, resolve the question with the coating supplier before the job begins.

For repeat orders, keep the agreed masking instructions with the relevant part or batch documentation.

## 6. What Should Be Checked After Coating?

After coating, inspect the areas identified in the job specification.

Depending on the component, checks may include:

- Confirming that the designated areas are free of unwanted coating.
- Checking that the visible finish meets the agreed requirements.
- Checking critical dimensions where specified.
- Confirming that threads and fittings work as intended.
- Checking electrical contact areas when required by the design.

The checks should follow the agreed acceptance criteria. A visual inspection alone may not be enough for a critical fit or electrical function.

## Questions to Ask Before Sending Aluminium for Powder Coating

Use these questions when reviewing a new component:

1. Are there any threaded holes that must remain clear?
2. Are there any close-fitting surfaces or critical dimensions?
3. Does the part have electrical contact points?
4. Are masking areas marked clearly on the drawing?
5. Has the coating supplier confirmed the masking method?
6. Are the required checks agreed before production?

These questions help the team address functional requirements as well as appearance.

## Conclusion

Successful aluminium powder coating depends on more than selecting a colour. The component must also work as intended after finishing.

Identifying threads, contact areas and critical fitting surfaces before production helps the fabricator and coating supplier agree on a suitable masking plan.

For architectural aluminium profiles and other coated components, clear drawings and early communication are useful steps toward a finish that meets the project's requirements.
